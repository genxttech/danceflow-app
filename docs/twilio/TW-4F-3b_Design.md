# TW-4F-3b: prepaid SMS credits, Messaging Service and campaign (design)

Status 2026-10-19. Owner decisions: **prepaid SMS credits are the default billing model for every independent studio, with ONE prepaid balance per verified legal business (usage and cost attributed to the studio location it belongs to)**; the ten follow-up decisions of 2026-10-19 are recorded in section 7 and supersede any recommendation in sections 3 and 5 that differs. DanceFlow's parent Twilio account may be billed centrally, but each studio funds its own onboarding, registration, phone-number and messaging costs through DanceFlow. Twilio subaccount isolation (approved D2: one subaccount per legal business) is **not** independent billing: Twilio bills the parent. This document records the assessment, the model, what is built in slice **3b-0** (local, synthetic, not applied to any hosted database) and the decisions still needed. Nothing here contacts Twilio or Stripe.

## 1. What this changes for TW-4F-3b and the earlier slices

| Area | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Who pays | implied: DanceFlow / the owner's invoice | the **studio**, from settled prepaid credit, before any fee-bearing Twilio call |
| Spend cap (3a) | permission-like guard per legal entity ($150 for ConfiDance) | unchanged, and still **not permission to spend**: each real fee-bearing operation needs its own authorization at the published price, **and** funded credit |
| Authorization | admin typed an amount | the amount **is** the published customer price (no price, no authorization, no other amount) |
| Reservation | marked an authorization used | also **holds the funding studio's credit atomically**; capture charges it, release frees it (and voids the authorization) |
| Brand (B1) | stopped on no authorization | also stops on `insufficient_credit`; a created brand is captured exactly once; a definite refusal releases; an unknown outcome stays held |
| 3b campaign vetting, Messaging Service, number purchase and monthly rent | to be designed | each is a fee kind with its own price, authorization, reservation, capture and reconciliation |
| 4F-4 activation | checklist only | adds: credit funded for the first period, recurring approval recorded, usage debit path live |

## 2. Reuse of existing DanceFlow financial primitives

- **Stripe webhook idempotency:** `claim_payment_provider_event` (PAY-WH-1) already gives one atomic, leased claim per Stripe event id. Funding settlement will run inside that claim and additionally key the ledger on the Stripe payment (`topup:<payment_intent>`), so a replay at either layer credits nothing twice.
- **Platform Stripe account and customer:** studios already pay DanceFlow through Stripe Checkout on the platform account (SaaS billing, add-ons). Top-ups use the same account and customer, one-time `payment` mode, per request.
- **Patterns, not tables:** the append-only, idempotency-keyed, per-record ledger shape of `client_account_ledger` and the PAY refund reconciliation. Not reused as tables: `client_account_ledger` is a studio's money owed to its *clients*; `usage_events` / `usage_addon_entitlements` are metered allowances (quantities, not money). Mixing either with DanceFlow-to-studio funds would conflate two different ledgers, so the new ledger is separate (and money is integer cents).
- **Authorization and role helpers:** `_tw4_r1_is_platform_admin`, `_sms_onb_studio_role`, the one-shot confirmations of 3a.

## 3. Model (built in 3b-0)

- **Price list** (`studio_sms_price_list`): one active published price per kind (`brand`, `campaign_vetting`, `number`, `monthly`, `sms_usage`), customer price plus DanceFlow's internal expected provider cost. Prices are never edited: a change retires the old price and publishes a new one.
- **Credit account** (one per **legal business**, never per location): settled balance, open reservations, hold, low-balance threshold (default $10). `available = balance - reserved`, spendable only while not on hold and not negative.
- **Ledger** (append-only, per-studio sequence, idempotency key per entry, a database check on each kind's sign pattern): `topup_settled`, `topup_reversed`, `reserve`, `release`, `capture`, `usage_charge`, `usage_refund`, `adjustment`. Balance and reserved are the sum of its deltas; `tw4_credit_verify` proves the counters agree.
- **Funding:** `tw4_credit_topup_pending` records an unsettled payment (not spendable). Only `tw4_credit_settle_topup` (called from the settled-payment webhook) credits it, once per provider payment. A refund or lost dispute (`tw4_credit_reverse_topup`) takes credit back once per event and may drive the balance negative, which blocks all spending until the studio funds it again.
- **Fee-bearing step:** authorize (published price, cap) -> reserve (atomic hold of the funding studio's credit, idempotent per operation, serialized by row locks so concurrent runs and retries bind one authorization and hold the money once) -> Twilio call -> capture on success, release on a definite refusal, hold on an unknown outcome. A released authorization is void and a retry needs a new one.
- **Attribution by location:** registration, vetting, number and recurring fees and messaging usage all debit the business balance; each ledger entry, reservation, funding payment and provider charge names the location it belongs to. The database enforces this with a composite foreign key to the business's own location links, so a location of another business cannot be attributed (and no balance, authorization or reservation can cross businesses).
- **Messaging usage:** `tw4_credit_debit_usage` is one atomic, idempotent debit that refuses when funds are short (no overdraft); `tw4_credit_refund_usage` credits undelivered or failed messages once per key.
- **Reconciliation:** `tw4_credit_record_provider_charge` stores the actual Twilio charge once per reference and flags a variance against the published expected cost. **The studio is never re-debited for a variance** (fixed published prices); variances are DanceFlow's to review. Delayed usage lands as later `usage_charge`/`usage_refund` entries by key.
- **Studio view:** `sms_credit_overview`, `sms_credit_history` and `sms_price_list` (own studio, owner or administrator only; prices exclude provider cost; history excludes references and actors).
- **No automatic top-ups or recurring payments:** nothing stores a payment method for charging, schedules a charge or tops up on low balance. Credit arrives only from a settled, explicitly started payment. Low balance is a flag (`low_balance_flagged_at`, set once per dip, cleared on recovery); sending a warning is a notification slice.
- **Retention:** the credit tables reference studios with `ON DELETE RESTRICT`: a studio with financial history cannot be hard-deleted (the account-deletion flow needs an explicit anonymize-and-retain rule; see decisions).

## 5. Decisions needed before expanding scope

1. **Funding unit.** Implemented: entity-level fees debit the funding (founding) studio; usage debits the sending studio. Confirm, or choose per-entity pooling or a different funding studio per entity.
2. **Prices.** Customer price per kind (brand, vetting, number, monthly rent, per-segment SMS), margin over Twilio's cost, sales-tax handling, and whether fees are shown separately. The price list is empty until set; nothing can be authorized without it.
3. **Top-up design.** Fixed packs vs custom amounts, minimum/maximum balance, payment methods (cards settle immediately; ACH settles later and stays pending until `async_payment_succeeded`).
4. **Unused credit.** Refund policy and expiry. Prepaid-balance rules vary by state and need a legal check before any promise; default proposal: non-refundable except at DanceFlow's discretion on account closure, no expiry.
5. **Negative balance.** Today a negative balance only blocks new spending. Decide whether sending is also suspended (Twilio subaccount suspend), after how long, and the write-off rule for disputes lost after credit was used.
6. **Recurring charges (number rent, campaign monthly).** Per-period owner authorization (safest, matches "no unapproved recurring payments") vs a standing approval with an end date, and what happens when funds run out (release the number? suspend sending?).
7. **Provider variance and delayed usage.** Confirm DanceFlow absorbs variances against published prices and who reviews them.
8. **Low-balance warnings.** Threshold defaults and channel (in-app only until the marketing email freeze lifts, or transactional email).
9. **Studio deletion.** How the account-deletion flow treats financial history (retain-and-anonymize is the likely answer).
10. **ConfiDance pilot funding.** The $150 maximum stays and is not permission to spend. Fund it by a real settled payment through the new flow, or a recorded complimentary adjustment (admin, written reason)? Either way each real fee-bearing operation still needs its own explicit written authorization.

Still blocking real Twilio activity: **Q1b** (cross-account Primary profile assignment) and **Q1c** (webhook signing token). No hosted migration, deployment, authenticated Twilio call, paid provisioning or SMS is authorized by this document.

## 6. Opus financial-integrity review (2026-10-19) and what was done

No ledger-arithmetic, locking, tenant-isolation or rollback defects were found; the review verified: counters = ledger sums, shape check, replay semantics, no capture-after-release, lock order (no cycle), settled-only funding, the NULL-safe studio views (a first version of those views had a three-valued-logic hole that let any non-member read a balance; it was caught by the new tests before review and fixed with `coalesce`). Findings and outcomes:

- **Stuck reservations (High):** a reservation from an earlier generation, a cancelled setup or a crash could never be captured or released. Fixed: `tw4_credit_resolve_reservation` (platform admin, written reason, any generation, idempotent) with `tw4_credit_open_reservations`; the runner now captures a fee step that already succeeded (a lost settlement is retried by the next run) and releases a reservation when the begin is refused (closed window, brand already exists).
- **Reversal/refund idempotency (Medium):** reversal keys now include the payment and require the provider event id; usage refunds must reference a recorded usage charge of the same studio and can never exceed it in total.
- **Unused authorization (Low):** `tw4_spend_withdraw`; failed payments can be marked failed (`tw4_credit_topup_failed`) and leave `pending`; reserve/settle validate the fee kind; internal ledger helpers are not callable by the service role; the migration refuses to apply over 3a `reserved` events it cannot bind.
- **Accepted for now:** an authorization is not bound to one location: the oldest authorized event of the kind in the legal entity is used and the funding studio is debited (decision 1 in section 5; the approver is always a platform admin and every run needs a platform admin). Credit accounts are created lazily by threshold/hold changes (harmless, but they prevent hard-deleting the studio).
- **Stack note:** `ON DELETE RESTRICT` means e2e fixtures and any studio-deletion flow cannot remove a studio with credit history; the fixtures leave such studios behind and `e2e/README.md` gives the local cleanup.

## 7. Owner decisions of 2026-10-19 (authoritative)

1. **One prepaid balance per verified legal business, attributed by location.** Implemented by re-keying the credit tables by legal entity (the first version, keyed by studio with a funding studio, was judged incompatible and corrected before merge). Owners of any location see the business balance and every entry with its location; locations of another business see nothing. No cross-entity funding or approval reuse: authorizations, reservations, payments and charges are entity-scoped in the database.
2. **Pricing:** cost-plus proposal with current provider costs and separately disclosed fees: `TW-4F-3b_Pricing_Proposal.md`. Exact prices stay pending owner approval; the price list is empty until then.
3. **Top-ups:** $25 / $50 / $100 packs only, cards first, **$500 balance ceiling** (balance + pending + the new pack), enforced when checkout is created; no automatic top-ups. A payment that has already settled is never refused.
4. **No routine expiry.** Final refund, closure and unclaimed-property terms wait for legal review; nothing in the model promises refunds.
5. **Insufficient funds:** new fee-bearing operations and outgoing chargeable SMS are blocked (`insufficient_credit` / `TW007`); compliance-critical opt-out handling must keep working with a zero balance (to be honoured when sending is built: STOP/START/HELP replies must not depend on credit); delayed usage arrives as later ledger entries by key and is reconciled, never retroactive edits.
6. **Recurring deductions** (number rent, campaign monthly fee) from prepaid credit only after a clear studio-owner authorization of the service, the price and spending limits; no monthly approval click within those limits; never an automatic card charge. Not built yet (next slice: owner-facing recurring authorization with price and period/total limits; a charge outside the authorization is refused). The low-balance threshold becomes the greater of $10 and the expected monthly charges.
7. **Published prices; DanceFlow absorbs ordinary variances** with the safeguards and limits proposed in the pricing document (section 3).
8. **Low balance:** greater of $10 or expected monthly charges; in-app alert first; transactional email considered separately. The flag is recorded in the ledger account (default threshold $10).
9. **Retention vs deletion:** legally required financial audit records are preserved; personal-data deletion and anonymization requests are honoured. The credit ledger holds no personal data (entity, location and actor identifiers, amounts, provider references), so the account-deletion flow must anonymize the people and keep these rows; the `ON DELETE RESTRICT` references stay until that slice defines the rule (a studio with credit history cannot be hard-deleted meanwhile).
10. **ConfiDance founder-authorized pilot exception:** no required prepaid deposit, a **$150 cumulative cap**, a separate explicit owner approval before every real fee-bearing Twilio action, and no generalized prepaid bypass. Implemented (after the first version was corrected, see section 9) as `tw4_pilot_exception_create`: a **non-spendable ceiling**, not credit. It creates no ledger entry, no balance, no payment record and no revenue; it exists once in the whole system, only for the verified ConfiDance Unlimited, LLC legal business, created by a platform admin with a written founder-authorization reference, never above $150 and never above the entity spend cap. A fee-bearing reservation of that business may be funded by the ceiling only when settled credit cannot cover it, only with its own operation-specific authorization (a spend event at the published price with the approving admin and approval reference, audited), cumulatively counting reservations still outstanding AND captured ones (a released one frees its share), serialized under the entity and account row locks so concurrent and retried operations cannot exceed it, and never for messaging usage. An account on hold is never funded.

Still open: legal terms (decision 4), recurring-authorization design (decision 6), transactional email (decision 8), anonymization rule (decision 9), the Stripe checkout/webhook and UI slices, and the real-provider blockers **Q1b** and **Q1c**.

## 8. Focused Opus review of the entity-keyed correction (2026-10-19)

Verdict: no cross-entity movement of money, balance, reservation, authorization or attribution (composite foreign keys to the business's own location links plus the posting check); locking, idempotency, ceiling, rollback and grants verified. Findings fixed in this slice: a payment marked failed that Stripe later settles is now credited once (the money was taken; nothing goes back to pending); the entity row is locked `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` so ledger inserts (which need `KEY SHARE`) cannot deadlock against reservations and pilot grants; refund totals are matched on the original ledger entry, not a truncated key; withdrawals write an audit record; the pilot exception exists once in the whole system (see section 9: it is a non-spendable ceiling, not a grant); `this_location` is never null; provider-charge replays compare the location; the migration refuses to apply over open 3a authorizations (they pre-date published prices). Documented, not changed: a first-time settled payment without a pending checkout skips the pack-size rule on purpose (a settled payment is never refused); studio administrators see sibling-location entries of their own business (the balance is shared by design); a location with credit history cannot be hard-deleted until the anonymization rule (decision 9) is built.

## 9. Pilot exception correction (2026-10-19)

The first version of decision 10 posted a `pilot_grant` credit of up to $150 into the ledger. That was wrong for the approved policy: it created spendable credit without a settled payment (usable for messaging usage with no per-operation authorization) and could be created for any business by any admin. Corrected before merge: the grant kind, function and ledger path are removed; the exception is the non-spendable ceiling described in section 7 item 10, reservations carry their funding source (`credit` or `pilot`), pilot-funded reservations move no ledger money, and every pilot-funded operation is traceable to its spend event, approval reference, approving admin, audit rows and reservation. Evidence: `localStack.tw4f3a.test.ts` (ceiling, authorization, cumulative and concurrent limits, no credit, no usage) and `localStack.tw4f3b0.test.ts` (not available to other businesses or non-admins; the ledger can no longer represent a free grant).

Second-pass hardening of the pilot exception (same day, after the focused Opus review): the funding source is chosen by the APPROVER on each authorization (`credit` or `pilot`) and recorded on the spend event, so there is no silent fallback from credit to the ceiling; a pilot-funded authorization is bound to one location, needs a written approval reference of 10+ characters and the existing exception, and an approval reference authorizes one operation (unique per business while the authorization is open or reserved); a pilot-funded reservation is refused while the account is on hold or negative; the pilot business spend cap cannot be raised above $150 while the exception exists, so ConfiDance total pilot spend (credit-funded and pilot-funded together) stays within the cap; creation requires exactly one legal business of that name. Accepted and flagged for the owner: the exception is bound to the legal name of the verified business (not to its EIN digits, which are private); pin the entity id when the real ConfiDance entity exists.

Third pass (re-review): a location-bound authorization is preferred over an unbound one for that location's operation; the pilot exception requires the business spend cap to be at most $150 at creation (the cap guard fires only on cap changes); the rollback re-applies the service-role-only privileges of the restored 3a authorize function (proved on the local stack); an approval reference authorizes one operation (so one owner approval cannot silently cover two different fees). Accepted: look-alike punctuation inside an approval reference is not normalized (the reference is evidence for a human, not an identity).

## 10. TW-4F-3b-pay: prepaid credit purchases through Stripe (2026-10-19)

**Built (local, synthetic only; nothing applied to DEV or PROD, nothing deployed).** Migration `20261123090000` (+ rollback), `src/lib/billing/smsCreditPurchases.ts`, `POST /api/billing/sms-credits/checkout`, and a credit handler at the head of the existing platform webhook (`/api/payments/webhook`).

- **Packs and account.** $25 / $50 / $100 only (enforced in TypeScript and in SQL). Stripe Checkout `payment` mode, card only, on the platform account. No saved payment method, no customer, no `setup_future_usage`, no subscription, no off-session charge: there is no automatic top-up path anywhere.
- **Who can buy.** An owner or administrator of a location whose legal business is verified; the role is re-checked in the database (`tw4_credit_checkout_context`). The credit lands in that business's single balance and the ledger entry names the buying location. The business is derived from the database, never from the request.
- **Spendable only after verified settlement.** A purchase is registered pending when the Checkout Session is created. The webhook (platform secret, signature and timestamp verified, PAY-WH-1 claim for replay protection) settles it only if the session is registered, `payment_status` is paid, and mode, currency, amount, business and location all match the registration. Credit is posted once under the key `topup:<session>`, so replays, redeliveries under a new event id and concurrent `completed` + `async_payment_succeeded` deliveries credit once. A session the system never registered credits nothing, whatever its metadata says; Connect-scoped events are never credit events.
- **$500 ceiling.** `balance + live pending + new pack` must be at most $50,000 cents, serialized by the account row lock, so concurrent checkouts cannot both pass. A pending checkout is live until its expiry (35 minutes; Stripe's minimum session life is 30). A payment that has already settled is never refused.
- **Refunds and disputes.** Refunds apply Stripe's cumulative `amount_refunded` (order independent, idempotent, never decreasing). A dispute takes back up to the still-unreversed amount (capped, never an endless retry), once per dispute id; a won dispute or `funds_reinstated` returns exactly what that dispute took. A dispute inquiry (`warning_*`) moves nothing. The balance may go negative, which blocks all spending until funded again (3b-0 rule).
- **Pilot separation.** The ConfiDance $150 pilot ceiling is a separate, non-spendable authorization (section 9). Nothing in this slice reads or writes it, and no function mentions it; no balance transfer or cross-business funding exists.

### Opus review (2026-10-19) and what was done
Fixed: (HIGH) webhook hook ran lookups on every platform charge/refund/dispute event, so deploying the app before the migration would have failed those legacy flows: a lookup against a database without the credit functions now means "not ours" and the legacy handlers run; other lookup errors still retry. (HIGH) Stripe requires Checkout expiry of at least 30 minutes after creation: request is now 35. (MEDIUM) a dispute inquiry no longer removes credit. (MEDIUM) a dispute on an already refunded payment is capped instead of failing forever. (MEDIUM, second review) a refund arriving after a dispute took the whole payment is now recorded (the excess moves from the dispute share to the refund share) and a won dispute returns only what was not refunded; a reinstatement replay is recognised before its amount is recomputed. Known gaps (latent, Stripe allows one dispute per charge): with several disputes on one payment a late refund is taken from their shared share; PostgREST may answer PGRST202 for a moment right after the migration (flagged charges are retried, disputes/refund objects fall to the legacy handlers). (LOW) the rollback also refuses while live pending checkouts exist. Accepted/documented: a refund that Stripe later lowers (failed refund) does not restore credit (conservative; reconcile manually); one location's owner/admin can hold pending checkouts that count toward the business ceiling for up to 35 minutes; a paid session that fails the registration match returns 500 until it expires (fail closed, no automatic alert yet).

## 12. Prepaid-credit payment-integrity hardening (2026-10-19)

Migration `20261125090000` (+ rollback), `smsCreditPurchases.ts`, the checkout route. Local/synthetic only; nothing applied to DEV or PROD.

- **Hold enforced server-side.** `tw4_credit_checkout_context` refuses a held business with `TW007` (the spending hold's own error) before any Stripe session is created, and `tw4_credit_topup_pending` re-checks under the account lock (a hold placed in between wins and the session is expired). The context returns `hold`, and the application requires it to be exactly `false`: a missing field, an older function or any odd answer fails closed. The route answers 423 `on_hold`. Authorization is decided first (a front-desk user still gets 403). The hold belongs to the legal business, so every location is refused. A payment that was already made is still settled while a hold is on (the money was taken); the credit stays unspendable until the hold is lifted. A pending checkout registered before the hold can still be paid.
- **Disputes have their own state** (`studio_sms_credit_disputes`: the claim, what was actually taken back, outcome). A withdrawal is capped to what is unreversed but the full claim is remembered; a reinstatement returns at most `payment - refunds - what the payment's other open disputes claim` (and never more than the disputed share), once per dispute id, in any event order. A reinstatement that arrives before its withdrawal is refused (22023) so Stripe retries it. This closes the documented multiple-dispute gap (a late refund taken from a shared share could be handed back by winning another dispute) and the case of a dispute whose withdrawal was capped to nothing. Property test: 60 random orders of refunds, disputes, wins and duplicates never retain more credit than payment minus refunds minus the disputes not won.
- **Known, safe under-credit.** If a dispute's withdrawal was held back by another open dispute and that other dispute is later also won, the first is not topped up again; an administrator adjustment corrects it. Stripe allows one dispute per charge, so this needs unusual provider behaviour.
- **Dependencies.** Requires `20261123090000`. The migration refuses (55000) if legacy dispute records cannot be reconstructed (a dispute capped to nothing, or several disputes on one payment); none exist anywhere hosted. App-before-migration: the new route code requires `hold === false`, so it fails closed (503) against the old context function until the migration is applied; purchases are off anyway. Rollback: app first, then the rollback (refuses once dispute records exist).

## 13. Recurring SMS service-charge authorization (2026-10-19)

Migration `20261126090000` (+ rollback), `smsRecurring.ts`, `/api/billing/sms-recurring/{authorize,revoke}`, a "Monthly texting charges" card on Settings → Texting and a read-only recurring section on `/platform/sms`. Local/synthetic only; nothing applied, enabled or scheduled.

- **What is authorized.** An **owner** of a location (not an administrator) authorizes phone-number rent (`number`) or the campaign monthly fee (`monthly`) at the **currently published price**, monthly, for a term of 1-12 months; the total spending limit is always price × term (the term is the control, so every month of the term can be charged and none beyond it). The database refuses an acknowledged price that is not the current published one (`TW008`), a second active authorization for the same location and fee, and any other fee kind. Authorizing and revoking are **service-role-only** database functions (`tw4_recurring_authorize`, `tw4_recurring_revoke`) called by the two routes with the signed-in user's id, so no studio user can reach them directly and the feature flag cannot be bypassed through the API; the database re-checks that the user is an owner of that studio. The row records price, term, limit, the exact acknowledgement (**written by the database** from the validated numbers; the owner reads the identical text, rendered from the same builder, in the panel before agreeing; a caller cannot supply it), who, when and `terms_version = terms-pending-approval`. A term is N whole UTC service months, the month of authorization first, ending on a month boundary, so exactly N charges fit. Acknowledged terms can never be edited; only `active -> revoked`.
- **What is charged, and from what.** Only `tw4_recurring_charge` (service role; the scheduler, never a studio) takes money, once per UTC month per authorization, from the legal business's **settled prepaid credit**, as an atomic reservation + capture under the account lock, attributed to the authorizing location (composite foreign key). It never charges a card, never tops up credit, and **never uses the ConfiDance pilot ceiling** (credit-funded only, no fallback; any pilot-funded operation still needs its own per-operation platform-admin approval through `tw4_spend_authorize`). Each attempt is recorded (`charged | unfunded | held | refused` with a reason) and audited. `unfunded` and `held` are retried (same record, attempts counted) and become `charged` when credit arrives or the hold is lifted.
- **Cannot bypass.** Refused when revoked, outside the term, price changed since authorization (the owner must authorize again), authorized total reached, the period was already charged under another authorization (revoke + re-authorize never double-charges), and **while the credit account is on hold**. Only the current or previous UTC month can be charged (the scheduler asks for both, oldest first, so an unpaid month is retried once credit arrives). A unique index on (business, location, fee, period) among charged rows is the last backstop; the account row lock serializes charges, top-ups, holds and revocations (no deadlock path: one lock order).
- **Low balance and suspension.** `sms_recurring_overview` tells owners/admins what is coming (expected monthly total, months covered, "available credit is below the next charges"), the hold state and the service state. `tw4_recurring_service_state` (service role) returns `none | active | past_due | suspended`: an unpaid, still-collectible service month (the current or the previous one, within the term) is `past_due` for 7 days (from the later of the month start and the authorization) and then `suspended`, so a missed month is not forgiven by the calendar turning; the overview also says why (`service_issue`: unfunded, held, limit, price) so the owner is told the right next step. The stored low-balance threshold stays administrator-controlled; the recurring-aware warning ("available credit is below the next monthly charges", months covered) is computed in the overview from the authorizations that can still charge. A month counts as paid if any authorization of the same location and fee was charged for it (revoke + authorize again never makes a paid month look overdue), and a refused period is final and is not retried. **Suspension is only a state for the sender to consult. Nothing in this slice releases, cancels or deletes a phone number**, and the screen says the numbers are kept.
- **Not built (dependencies).** The scheduler that calls `runDueRecurringCharges` (no cron or route is registered), the sender's use of the service state, notification emails for low balance, and any studio-facing change history beyond the activity list. Final retail prices and legal terms remain pending approval; the statement says so and `terms_version` is a placeholder.
- **Product decisions to confirm.** Grace period (7 days); first charge in the month of authorization with no proration; owner-only authorization; whether recurring charges should also count against the entity spend cap (currently bounded by each authorization's own limit and the credit); whether an authorization may run longer than 12 months.
- **Dependencies / flags.** Requires `20261125090000`. `SMS_RECURRING_AUTHORIZATION=on` enables the two routes (default off, 404) and shows the card when nothing exists yet. The app is safe before the migration (the card is hidden when the overview cannot be read).
- **Opus review (2026-10-19).** No path found that charges without authorization, from the wrong business, or with the pilot. Fixed: the term/limit mismatch that put every authorization into a `suspended` tail (calendar-aligned terms); suspension that lifted when the month turned and unpaid months never retried; authorize/revoke callable directly through the API with a caller-supplied acknowledgement (now service-role-only and database-written); cause-blind "Add credit" messages and "Texting is paused" wording (now cause-specific and honest that nothing consumes the state yet); UTC-only date arithmetic; the low-balance threshold (now a computed warning; the administrator-set threshold is left alone), a paid month shown overdue after revoke + re-authorize, partial limits that left a suspended tail (the limit is now price × term), and refused periods retried every run. Open decision: whether recurring charges should also count against the entity spend cap (ConfiDance's cap is $150 while the exception exists; recurring charges are bounded only by each authorization's own limit and the credit).

## 14. Explicit release risks and proposed mitigations (prepaid credit)

**R1. A pending checkout can still be paid after a hold is placed.** A Checkout Session registered before the hold stays payable for up to 35 minutes; its payment settles (the money was taken) and the credit sits unspendable until the hold is lifted. Impact: a customer pays into an account DanceFlow has frozen (for example after a dispute or a suspected fraud). Today: no new session can be created during a hold (database-enforced), settlement is never blocked, the hold blocks spending and recurring charges. Proposed mitigations, in order: (1) when a hold is placed, expire the business's open Checkout Sessions (a small platform action listing pending purchases and calling Stripe `checkout.sessions.expire`, then marking them failed); (2) a runbook for a payment that settled during a hold: refund it through Stripe, which reverses the credit through the cumulative-refund path (no manual ledger edit); (3) a monitoring query/alert for "settled while on hold" next to the existing stale-pending view; (4) a product decision on whether such payments are refunded automatically. Not implemented; accepted as a documented risk because purchases stay off until launch is approved.

**R2. Several disputes on one payment (unusual).** Stripe normally allows one dispute per charge. If a payment ever has several, a withdrawal is capped to what is unreversed, each dispute's full claim is remembered, and a win returns at most `payment - refunds - the other open claims`, so credit can never exceed what DanceFlow actually keeps. The cost is a conservative **under-credit**: if two disputes are both won after a refund, part of the credit can stay debited. Mitigations: an alert when a payment has more than one dispute record, and a documented administrator adjustment (`tw4_credit_adjust` with a written reason) after the disputes close.

**R3. Recurring charges are not scheduled yet.** Until the scheduler and the sender's use of the service state exist, authorizations record consent only; nothing is charged and nothing is suspended. Mitigation: do not enable `SMS_RECURRING_AUTHORIZATION` before the scheduler slice ships and is reviewed.

**R4. Prices and legal terms are pending approval.** The authorization is bound to the price the owner saw; a price change refuses charges until the owner authorizes again. Mitigation: publish prices and terms (and update `terms_version` and the statement text) before enabling.

## 15. Recurring-charge execution and sender suspension (2026-10-19)

Migration `20261127090000` (+ rollback), `smsRecurring.ts` (scheduler entry point), `/api/cron/sms-recurring`, and the outbound sender gate in `studioSender.ts`. Local/synthetic only; nothing applied, no scheduler registered, nothing enabled.

- **Spending limits (owner direction).** Recurring charges count toward the legal business's spending cap: `_tw4f3a_committed` now adds recurring charges taken to authorized + reserved spend events, so setup-fee authorizations and recurring charges draw on ONE cap. A recurring authorization needs the WHOLE term to fit under the cap beside what the business's other active authorizations may still charge (`_tw4rc_promised`: total limit minus charged; `TW004` otherwise: choose a shorter term; the acknowledgement says the charge "fits within my business's spending limit") and every charge re-checks: past the cap it is **held** (`legal-business spending cap reached`, recorded, retried once the cap is raised or a setup authorization is withdrawn). The owner is told "spending limit reached", not "add credit". The cap is cumulative over the business's lifetime (captured fees stay committed), which is existing behaviour that recurring charges now share (**product decision for the owner: confirm the cap is lifetime and that administrators raise it routinely, or define a rolling/per-term cap**; setup-fee authorizations do not yet look ahead at promised recurring terms); a business whose recurring spend passes $150 can no longer be given the pilot exception, and the cap cannot be lowered below what is committed. Locks: the entity row first (share lock in authorize, no-key-update in charge, the order `tw4_spend_reserve` uses), then the account row, so a setup-fee authorization and a recurring charge cannot both pass the cap and neither can race pilot-exception creation or a cap change.
- **The pilot business (ConfiDance).** A business with the pilot exception cannot hold a standing recurring authorization (`TW011`; this migration also revokes any that exist), a charge for one is **refused** and final, and the service state ignores a pilot business entirely (so it can never be suspended by authorizations it cannot be charged for): each recurring fee-bearing operation there needs its own explicit approval (`tw4_spend_authorize`, funding `pilot`, one location, written reference). No schedule can consume the pilot ceiling or stand in for those approvals; SMS usage stays outside the ceiling (usage debits only from credit). The studio screen explains this and shows no authorize form.
- **Independent studios.** Unchanged and stricter: credit only, settled, correct business, under the authorization and the cap; fail closed on insufficient credit, expiry, revocation, hold or cap; never a card, never a top-up.
- **Scheduler.** `runScheduledRecurringCharges` takes the database's single run lease (`tw4_recurring_run_begin`; refused when the **database kill switch is off (default)**, when another run holds the lease, or when the lease is stale and replaced), charges the previous and the current UTC month oldest first under the run id, and records the outcome (`tw4_recurring_run_finish`: counts, errors; a failed charge call marks the run failed and the next run retries). Every charge carries the run id and the database **fences** a runner whose lease expired or was replaced, and halts a run when the database switch is turned off (`22023`); both are checked after the entity/account locks are held. (A charge without a run id is the service-role repair path: it still honours every authorization, cap, hold and funding rule. A fenced runner cannot double-charge in any case: the per-period unique key and the ledger idempotency keys are the backstop.) Each charge attempt is stamped with its run (`run_id`); runs and switch changes (platform admin, written reason) are append-only. Overlapping invocations are harmless (one runs, the others report busy). The route `/api/cron/sms-recurring` answers 404 unless `SMS_RECURRING_SCHEDULER=on`, then needs the cron secret, then the database switch; it returns counts only. **It is not in `vercel.json`**: nothing calls it on a schedule.
- **Reconciliation.** Every charged recurring row must have a captured credit reservation and a capture ledger entry of the same business, location, fee and amount; `_tw4rc_reconciliation_gaps()` counts exceptions in both directions (a charged row without its capture, and a captured recurring reservation or capture entry without a charged row) and the platform view shows the count, the switch, the latest runs and what was not charged.
- **Sender suspension.** `resolveStudioSmsSender`, the only resolver for outbound texts, asks `tw4_recurring_service_state` after the registration checks: `suspended` blocks new outbound texts (manual: 503 with a clear message; automated appointment texts: skipped with `sms_service_suspended`), an unreadable or unrecognized answer **fails closed** (manual 500 / automated delivery fails), and `none | active | past_due` send. Where the function is not installed (this code shipped before its migration) nothing changes. **Inbound is untouched**: `resolveStudioFromInboundSender` never consults the state, and STOP / START / HELP are handled (and answered by Twilio Advanced Opt-Out) exactly as before for a suspended business. Suspension still never releases a phone number.

An authorization whose term has ended is closed (with an audit row) when the owner authorizes the same fee again, so a renewal is never blocked by a dead row.

### Outstanding release gates (this slice)
1. A release owner must deliberately add the cron schedule (`vercel.json`), set `SMS_RECURRING_SCHEDULER=on` and `SMS_RECURRING_AUTHORIZATION=on`, and switch the database switch on (`tw4_recurring_set_switch`): four independent gates, all off.
2. The ISV sending path for businesses with a recurring authorization is not otherwise wired (the sender gate protects the existing per-studio registration path); a business with an authorized number but no recurring authorization is not governed by this state.
3. Prices and legal terms (and `terms_version`) pending approval; notification emails for low balance / suspension; the `none` state's meaning for businesses that never authorized.
4. Migration order: ... `20261126090000` then `20261127090000`; the app tolerates the state function being absent (sends unchanged) but the cron route and the new admin view need the migration.
5. The release risks R1/R2 above remain.

## 16. Monthly spending limits replace the lifetime cap for routine costs (2026-10-19)

Migration `20261128090000` (+ rollback), `smsMonthlyLimits.ts` and client, `/api/billing/sms-limits/{set,timezone}`, `SmsLimitPanel`. Local/synthetic only; nothing applied to DEV or PROD, nothing enabled (`SMS_MONTHLY_LIMITS` off). Supersedes the "one shared cap" rule of section 15.

**Owner decisions implemented (D1-D6, 2026-10-19).**
- **D1 Setup budget.** One-time registration, vetting and provisioning fees stay under the cumulative `spend_cap_cents` budget (`_tw4f3a_committed`, spend events only), which never resets. The ConfiDance $150 pilot ceiling and its per-operation approvals are unchanged. Recurring charges NO LONGER count toward it (D6).
- **D2 Timezone.** An IANA zone per legal business (`studio_sms_entity_timezones`, append-only, keyed by effective month), inherited from the founding location (first linked). An owner or platform admin changes it; the change takes effect from the NEXT calendar month, with an audit row and a reason. Month windows tile the timeline across a change (the start of month K is local midnight of the 1st in the zone in force for K), so no instant is in two months or none.
- **D3 Changes.** Decreases apply at once. Increases also apply at once but need the owner to have signed in within 15 minutes (`TW014`), no hold and a non-negative balance (`TW007`); the platform path needs a written reason. The confirmation shows the current spend and the proposed limit; the change row records spend at the time. A limit may be scheduled up to 12 months ahead; the latest decision for a month wins.
- **D4 Defaults.** The limit is $0 until set (`TW012`: fail closed). The platform safety ceiling starts at $500 a month; a higher ceiling for one business needs a separate platform approval row; the effective limit is the lesser of limit and ceiling. The ceiling is a bound, never an authorization.
- **D5 SMS usage.** Usage debits already go through `tw4_credit_debit_usage`, which now enforces the monthly limit and the funds, atomically, under the account lock (every path that changes a limit, a reservation or the balance takes the same lock). Wiring the sender to pre-send reservations, with evidence-based reconciliation and no automatic refund of uncertain provider outcomes, is a separate bounded slice and is **mandatory before real SMS activation**.
- **D6 Accounting.** A month's spend is attributed by ledger `created_at`: usage net of refunds, recurring captures (`capture:rec:%`, `capture:commit:%`) and open commitment/recurring reservations. Setup captures are excluded. A recurring capture belongs to the month its funds were RESERVED in, so a set-aside captured by a later month's charge is counted once, in the month it was set aside, and needs no new room (verified by the Opus review, which found the double count in an earlier draft).

**First-month commitment.** Authorizing a recurring charge needs room under the month's limit and settled funds, and sets the first month aside as reservation `commit:<id>` (available credit drops, so setup and usage cannot consume it). The first charge captures that same reservation (no second reserve); revoking, or the end of the term (`tw4_recurring_release_ended`, run by the scheduler first), releases it. Later months reserve and capture as before, and a charge that would pass the month's limit is recorded as `held` and retried when the limit rises or the month turns.

**Pilot business.** Usage needs a monthly limit like any business and is paid from credit only, never from the pilot; a standing recurring authorization is still refused (`TW011`).

**Scheduler.** The code no longer computes periods: `tw4_recurring_due_list` returns (authorization, period) pairs for each business's current and previous month in its own timezone.

**Migration order and rollback.** `...25` (credit) -> `...26` (recurring authorization) -> `...27` (recurring execution) -> `...28` (this). The precondition refuses without `...27` or if already applied. The rollback restores the section-15 functions and drops the new tables; it REFUSES while any limit, ceiling, timezone change or set-aside commitment exists (clear them deliberately; roll back the app first, then the SQL).

**Release gates.** (1) D5 sender integration before any real SMS. (2) The tw4f3b0/tw4f3bpay suites now set a limit in their fixtures: a PROD business that debits usage needs one first. (3) The `SMS_MONTHLY_LIMITS` flag and the existing four recurring gates stay off. (4) Follow-up: the brand low-volume tier price. (5) Low-balance warnings and suspension emails remain a separate slice.

**Opus review (2026-10-19) and what changed.** Fixed: a set-aside is captured only while settled credit still stands behind it (else the charge is recorded `unfunded` and the set-aside kept); recurring captures are attributed to their reservation's month; an administrator can no longer capture or release a `commit:` reservation by hand (revoking releases it); a timezone change whose new zone would already have begun next month starts the month after, so the current month never moves; ledger/reservation indexes on (business, created_at) for the per-debit month query; the rollback re-revokes `_tw4rc_promised`; the limit panel shows dates in the business's timezone.

**Known limitations, accepted for this slice.**
- A set-aside for an authorization that can never be charged (price changed, or a pilot exception created later) stays reserved until the owner revokes or the term ends.
- A one-month term authorized after the month's last scheduler run is not charged (its term ended); the set-aside is released.
- The recent-sign-in check uses the user's last sign-in, not the individual session.
- Scheduled future limits are validated (hold, balance, sign-in) when scheduled, not when they take effect; an emergency decrease "now" does not cancel a higher limit already scheduled for a later month (the panel shows next month's limit).
- `tw4_recurring_release_ended` is not fenced by the scheduler lease (it is idempotent and takes each business's lock in turn).
